# CourtVisionIQ 2.0 — Pre-Train Review

> Written 2026-09-09, after the last workstream merged and before the 2.0 train. This is a
> **theory review**: what 2.0 changed, what the build actually found, whether better results
> should be expected and where, and what to do before and after the train. Every claim is tagged
> **[measured]** (a number in the tracker or reproduced here) or **[inferred]** (reasoning from
> the code and the mechanism). Sources: `docs/v2_progress.md` (the tracker, corrections A–U),
> `docs/v2_planned_changes.md` (the spec), the five build sessions of 2026-09-03 → 09-09, and the
> code on `feature/version2` at `ed5f800`.

The single most important fact for reading anything below: **no 2.0 number exists yet.** Every
claim about improvement is a prediction. The programme deliberately traded its one clean
measurement point (Gate A, a Phase-1-only eval on v1.0 weights) for time, so the first eval will
show the combined effect of thirteen changes and two ablation knobs are the only way to separate
them.

## 2. What 2.0 changed, by mechanism

It helps to sort the thirteen workstreams by *what kind of thing* each changes, because the kinds
have different expected effects and different failure modes.

| Kind | Workstreams | What it does to the system |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Controller rules** | 1, 2, 6 (timeout budget), 4 (FT count from token), 11d (Rule 3 gating) | Illegal states that v1 could sample become unreachable: fouls resolve to the right side, free throws go to the right team, nothing straddles a buzzer, substitutions happen only where the NBA rulebook allows them |
| **B. Training-data repair** | Gate B's two fixes (jump-ball binding, FT possessions), 11a (roster flicker), corrections P, Q, and §7's technicals recovery | v1.0 trained on data with these defects. 2.0 does not. See §3 — this is the under-appreciated half of the programme |
| **C. New information** | 3 (15 zones), 4 (`shooting 2pt`/`3pt`), 5 (fouled player), 6 (timeouts, team rebounds), 10b (possession clock), 11b (stint/minutes/fouls per player), 11c (bench bundle), and the game-state features consumed for the first time | Things the model could not see and had to absorb into player embeddings or leave to dials |
| **D. Architecture** | 9 (shared backbone), 10a (two local heads, window 8), 11d (`sub_decision` replaces `stint_length`) | One structural bet (locality) and one re-posed question (rotation as a decision, not a duration) |
| **E. Training signal** | 12 (loss mask: event head 10.6% → 31.5% of positions masked, time head also skips period breaks) | The heads stop learning answers to questions the simulator never asks |
| **F. Measurement** | 13 (per-quarter boxes, per-zone shot mix), the three TF-free gates | The train becomes readable |

Two things the spec promised that 2.0 does **not** contain: clutch loss weighting (rejected before
building, correction S — the right call, see §5.6) and the free-throw index feature (parked in v3
§1 pending a post-train check of make rate by position in the trip).

## 3. What the build actually found

The plan was "fix rules, add information." What the build mostly did, measured by where the time
went and what moved numbers, was **find defects in the data v1.0 was trained on.** None of these
was in the spec; all were found by one number checked against an independent source, and none by
a unit test.

| Defect | Scale [measured] | What it did to v1.0's training data |
|---|---|---|
| Jump-ball team binding (correction L) | both sides bound to one abbreviation in **~47% of games in every era** | Every timeout in those games mislabelled or dropped; **and** — this is new, derived here from `season_context.py` — the `home_team`/`away_team` columns feed `team_games` and `team_last`, so in a collapsed game one abbreviation is advanced twice and the other team's game is never recorded. The other team's next game then carries an inflated `days_rest` and a lagging `games_played`. Those are `TEAM_SCALAR_COLS`, fused into **every** head. [inferred from code; the pre-fix data no longer exists to measure it] |
| Roster snapshot flicker (11a) | ~21 phantom lineup changes per game against 46.5 real substitutions, a ~45% corruption of the on-court five | The roster encoder's input was wrong on those rows; minutes and stint derivations were poisoned before a rotation model could be built on them |
| Free-throw possession over-count (correction M) | +12% possessions in every era | Not in v1's data (the clock is new), but the v1-era *pace reference* carried the same 0.44·FTA bias (correction T), so v1's pace was tuned against a biased target |
| Dead-ball rows inside a trip (correction T) | ~7,500 trips a season split in two | Same |
| `Nene ` (correction P) | one player, two embeddings, every Denver game in 2002-03 desynced after his first substitution | Minor in isolation; it is how 11a's gate was proven sensitive |
| Sub row naming a player already on the floor (correction Q) | 12 games in 2002-03, one for 148 rows | Small |
| Standalone technicals never cleaned (§7) | 632 rows a season | Data v1 never saw |
| And-1s (correction I) | **24%** of shooting fouls match neither `shooting 2pt` nor `3pt` | Would have been a labelling hole, caught before the clean |

**Why this matters for the forecast.** Category B improvements are free in a way category C is
not: they need no capacity, no new dials and no learning — the same architecture simply stops
being trained on wrong inputs. The team-context corruption is the one to underline. The season
context features were added in a v1 train precisely to give the model rest and schedule
information, and in nearly half the games the team half of that information was wrong. No one
has measured what v1.0 learned from it because the defective data is gone; but a feature that is
random noise 47% of the time is a feature the model learns to ignore, so v2 is plausibly the first
train in which team rest and games-played can carry signal at all.

## 4. Should we expect better results, and where?

The honest frame first. The project's own yardsticks, from `full4-s100` and the baseline check:

| Metric | v1.0 `full4-s100` | Noise / floor |
|---|---|---|
| win pick / Brier | 63% / 0.2173 | pick moves ±3–4 pts between runs at n=100; market Brier ≈ 0.19 |
| team points MAE | 9.26 | a perfect conditional mean would still score ~8: game-level scoring noise is ~10 pts sd |
| player minutes MAE | 5.66 | not near a floor |
| player points MAE | 4.83 | not near a floor |
| model vs season-to-date baseline (rotation PTS) | +6.6% worse, 47% paired win rate — **measured at `full1`, never re-run** | the real scoreboard |

**Team-level metrics are near their noise floor; player-level metrics are not.** That is where to
look for 2.0's effect, and it is also why per-player MAE against the season-average baseline is the
right headline: it is the metric with room to move and the one the pitch depends on.

One correction to the project's own folklore, because it changes what to expect. The memory
records that "a generative simulator's job is to create game-to-game variance, which inflates
per-game MAE." That would be true if the model's prediction were *one* sampled game. It is not:
the eval scores the **mean over 100 sims**, which is a low-variance point estimate exactly like a
season average. So the remaining +6.6% is not variance inflation. It is the model's conditional
mean being *worse* than a per-player constant — the model is not yet using roster, opponent and
game state better than "who this player is on average." That residual is bias and misallocation,
which is precisely what categories A, B and C attack. The gap is closable in principle.

### 4.1 Metric-by-metric forecast

Probabilities are my calibrated judgement, not a model output.

| Metric | Direction | Confidence | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Player minutes MAE** (5.66) | improves, plausibly to the 4.5–5.2 band | ~70% | The stint timer needed a 1.30 multiplier because it regressed log-duration; the count head asks a well-posed question at the right denominator (20–27% of legal opportunities vs 1% of rows). Outgoing pick now sees stint seconds and fouls; incoming pick sees seconds-since-sat. Score difference is consumed for the first time, so garbage-time rotation is learnable. Risk: no dial on the count (§5.1) |
| **Per-player FTA / PF** | improves | ~75% | Free throws now go to the fouled player the head names (99.5% match to the real shooter [measured]); three-shot trips are learned rather than sampled from a head never trained for it (v1 awarded 3 FTs ~12× too often [measured, v1 spec]) |
| **eFG% / shot mix** (eFG −1.7 pts, 3PA +2.3 high) | bias shrinks pre-dial; `SHOT_RESULT_BIAS.made` should refit well below 0.40 | ~65% | The era table [measured] shows *per-zone* make rates are nearly stationary (`top3` 34.9% in 2003 and 2023; `corner3` 36.7→38.5) while the *mix* moves enormously (3PA share 18→39%). v1 forced one `shot_result` head to absorb both the mix shift and the rate, and the +0.40 logit correction is the scar. Fifteen zones move the era drift into `shot_type` (which has a season embedding) and leave `shot_result` a stationary problem |
| **Rebounds** (OREB +1.0 high) | bias shrinks | ~60% | Team rebounds are learned (12.8k a season) instead of a 0.10 coin flip that emitted no row; the rebound-type head sees where the miss came from |
| **Assists** (−1.95 low) | little change | — | Nothing in 2.0 targets the assist share of the event mix. Watch `EVENT_BIAS` at refit; if assists need a large offset, the event head's mask (§5.3) is the first suspect |
| **Pace** | first raw reading uninformative | — | The time heads no longer train on the ~20% of positions at Δt≈0 (free throws inside a trip, the assisted shot, the block). Removing zero-gap rows from an MAE target shifts predictions up at the queried positions, so the clock will read *slower* than v1's under the same `DELTA_TIME_SCALE`. Refit is mandatory, not optional; the tracker already says so |
| **Win pick / Brier** | flat within noise | ~80% flat | Nothing in 2.0 adds team-strength information. Better star minutes help marginally. Do not read a 3-point move either way as signal |
| **Model vs season-to-date baseline** | crosses 50% paired win rate on minutes-driven stats (MIN, PTS, FTA, PF) after the dial refit | ~45% | Minutes are the multiplier on every per-player stat; if minutes MAE drops a full point, the rotation cohort's PTS MAE should follow. This is the number that decides the pitch, and it is close enough that it is worth running the baseline comparison *first* (§6.2) |

### 4.2 The dial package as a scorecard

v1.0 needed nineteen dials, several of them large: `SHOT_RESULT_BIAS.made +0.40`,
`TYPE_BIAS.foul_type["loose ball"] +1.2`, `STINT_LENGTH_SCALE 1.30`, `PLAYER_TEMPERATURE 2.0`.
Every dial is a place the model is wrong in a way the controller has to paper over. **The size of
the refit package is the cleanest single-number verdict on whether 2.0 understood the game
better**, and it is independent of holdout noise. Suggested targets, so the refit is graded and
not just performed:

| Dial | v1.0 | 2.0 target | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `SHOT_RESULT_BIAS.made` | +0.40 | < +0.15 | zones (§4.1) |
| `TYPE_BIAS.foul_type["loose ball"]` | +1.2 | ~0 | side-aware masking makes the type reachable where it happens |
| `EVENT_BIAS.foul` | +0.11 | ~0 | the event head no longer trains on continuation rows that distorted the mix |
| `STINT_LENGTH_SCALE`, `SUB_FATIGUE_WEIGHT`, `DEADBALL_REBOUND_PROB` | 1.30 / — / 0.10 | gone | replaced by learned heads |
| `PLAYER_TEMPERATURE` | 2.0 | ≤ 1.7 | already pencilled in for the next train; re-probe |

If the package comes back the same size as v1's, the workstreams did not land in the weights and
the ablations are the next step, not more dials.

## 5. Design risks worth naming before the train

### 5.1 The rotation head has no dial, and rotation is the metric we most need to move

`_TUNING_KEYS` lost four rotation dials and gained none. The count head's output is sampled from a
4-class softmax (81% zero, 13% one [measured]). If the trained head under- or over-substitutes by
even a few points per opportunity, that compounds over ~150–200 opportunities a game and there is
**no inference-time lever** except `SUB_MAX_GAP_SECONDS` (a backstop that fires at 600 s) and
`SUB_INCOMING_TEMPERATURE` (who, not how many). Recommendation in §6.1: a `SUB_COUNT_BIAS` logit
offset on the zero class, in `_TUNING_KEYS`, defaulting to 0. Twenty lines, and it turns a
possible retrain into a refit. Rule of the programme: build the lever before you need it.

### 5.2 The bench the head trains on is not the bench the sim offers

Training reads availability as "everyone who reached the floor in this game"; the simulator reads
it as the full roster handed in (`_bench_inputs`, matching `_avail_mask`). The tracker names this
as an inherited compromise. Its consequence is asymmetric: in the **eval**, the roster is extracted
from the real game, so the bench is exactly the players who played — the head is never asked to
reject a DNP. In **deployment**, a 15-man active list contains players the head has never seen in
a bench bundle, and its only defence is the `has_played` flag and the player embedding. The v1
symptom (rank-15+ players over-played by +4 to +14 minutes, patched with
`SUB_INCOMING_TEMPERATURE 0.45`) is this asymmetry. The eval will therefore be optimistic on
minutes relative to real use. Measure it: one extra eval with each game's full 13–15 man roster
instead of its participants, minutes MAE side by side. That number is the honest deployment figure.

### 5.3 The loss mask changes what the event head is calibrated on

Masking ~31% of positions is correct in principle (train where you are asked). Two consequences
to watch. First, the masked rows were the *easy* ones (assist→shot is deterministic), so the event
head's reported validation accuracy will drop from v1's 75.3% — that is expected and not a
regression. Second, the *input* history still contains those rows; only the loss skips them. The
head can still attend to a free-throw trip, it just stops predicting inside one. Fine. What would
be a problem is the sub-mask already present in v1 combining with the new one to remove too much
of some rare event's context — `timeout`, at ~12 a game, is the token to check for training-set
count after masking.

### 5.4 Local attention window versus the row-expanded stream

Eight rows was chosen with v1's grammar in mind. 2.0's stream is *denser* in some places (foul →
victim → up to three free throws with substitutions and timeouts interposed, observed depths of
six or more) and *sparser* in others (steals and offensive fouls collapsed to one row). A trip can
fill the entire window. Not a defect — the six global heads still see everything — but it means
the v3 §1 review trigger (free-throw make rate by position in the trip) is a direct test of whether
the local heads do what they were built for. Run it.

### 5.5 Thirteen changes, one train, two knobs

The ablation knobs are `LOCAL_ATTENTION_HEADS = 0` and `MASK_CONTINUATION_ROWS = False`, both
against the same preprocess. A full factorial is four trains; that is too many. Policy in §6.3.

### 5.6 The clutch decision was right, and the reason generalises

Dropping clutch weighting (correction S) is the best design decision in the programme, and not
because clutch does not matter. The programme is scored on whole-game box accuracy; a loss weight
that favours 5% of rows buys accuracy there by spending it on the other 95%, and the spec's own
text told the reader to watch for the harm. The general rule — *a knob whose documentation tells
you to watch for its own damage should not ship on* — should be applied to every dial in the refit.

### 5.7 Rollout cost

Every foul now draws a victim (+~40 head calls a game), the count head fires at every legal
opportunity (~150–200 forward passes a game, one per opportunity for both sides), and timeouts
add events. Expect eval throughput to fall by a noticeable fraction; the `--procs` pool and the
harvest loop still apply. Not a correctness risk, but budget for it on the pod.

### 5.8 Holdout pairing

The v2 verdict is only clean if it is a **paired** comparison against `full4-s100`: the same 100
game ids, 100 sims. The holdout is derived by the train from `FINAL_SEASON_FRACTION`; the re-clean
changed row counts but not game ids or order, so the ids should match. Check
`training/full_run_state.json` after the train against `results/v1.0/full4-s100/holdout.json`
before spending GPU hours on the eval.

## 6. Recommendations

### 6.1 Before the train (cheap, all TF-free)

1. **Push the branch.** 97 commits on one disk is the largest single risk in the repository and
   costs nothing to remove.
2. **Add `SUB_COUNT_BIAS`** (§5.1): a per-class logit offset dict on `predict_sub_count`, in
   `_TUNING_KEYS`, default `{}`, obeying the call-time-read contract `tests/test_dials.py` enforces.
3. **Record the v1.0 dial package** as the scorecard baseline (§4.2) — it is in
   `results/v1.0/full4-s100/dials.json` already; the target column is what to write down.
4. **Count the post-mask training positions per event token** from `train.npz`, to confirm no
   rare token (`timeout`, `flagrant`, `technical`) has been masked into scarcity.

### 6.2 After the train — the order of measurement matters

1. **Run the baseline comparison first**, before any dial is touched:
   `python -m reporting.baseline_comparison results/<model>/<run>`. It is read-only and it is the
   scoreboard. Not re-running it since `full1` is the programme's largest measurement gap.
2. Then the diagnostics in this order, each with its independent number already known:
   per-zone make rates against the §3 era table; per-quarter boxes (does a trailing team foul, does
   heave frequency sit in the last seconds of periods); the rotation table from 11a's gate
   (subs/team, top-10 minutes, 10+-minute players) — **which does not yet exist on the sim side**;
   `compare_holdout` has zone mix and Δt but no rotation section. Adding one is ~40 lines and it
   is the independent number for the largest workstream.
3. Refit dials from zero in the order pace → event mix → shot result → home court, one at a time,
   grading each against §4.2.
4. Re-run the baseline comparison. That pair of numbers — before and after the refit — is the
   result of 2.0.

### 6.3 Ablation policy — on evidence, not by default

- Train the full 2.0 first. No ablation until it is measured.
- `MASK_CONTINUATION_ROWS = False` only if the event-mix dials come back as large as v1's.
- `LOCAL_ATTENTION_HEADS = 0` only if the free-throw-by-position check (v3 §1) is flat where the
  real data is not, or if the possession-clock and local heads look redundant in a probe.
- Never both at once; the effects are not separable that way.

### 6.4 What v2.1 should be

- **Rolling team style priors** (v3 §3, "the cheap half"): pace, 3PA rate, FTA rate over each
  team's trailing ~20 games, computed in the same pass as season context, no external data. The
  model has **no team identity** today — a team is the five embeddings on the floor — and this is
  the cheapest way to give it one. It is also the only listed idea with a plausible effect on the
  team-strength metrics (win pick, Brier), which nothing in 2.0 touches.
- **Bench realism** (§5.2): decide whether deployment supplies a rotation list or the head learns
  to reject DNPs; measure first.
- **`_build_split` performance**: six copies of a 7.8-minute `np.where` scan, flagged in 11b and
  left. A fresh preprocess pays it six times.
- **One `python -m gates` entry point** wrapping the three TF-free checks (`zones`,
  `game_state_features`, `rotation_features`), so the pre-train checklist is one command and one
  exit code rather than three tables read by eye.
- **Symbol-anchored specs.** Line numbers in the spec went stale three times; the next spec should
  cite `module.symbol` only.

## 7. Process notes from the five sessions

Worth keeping, because they are what made the programme work and they are not obvious from the
code:

- **The tracker as handoff document** carried the programme across six cold-start sessions with no
  loss of state. The START HERE block, the corrections list and the "Result / Notes" slots per
  workstream are the pattern; keep it for 2.1.
- **"Decide the independent number before writing code"** was written down after Gate B and then
  honoured in 11a (five two ways), 12 (masked share by two routes), and 13 (period boxes sum to the
  whole). It found every real bug. The unit-test count (764) found none of them.
- **The same lesson recurred inside the branch that recorded it**, twice (`ARCH_KEYS` twice,
  `REQUIRED_HEADS` four times). The fix is structural — the list lives in `config` now — not
  behavioural.
- **One branch was merged on instruction without a test run** (§7 schema cleanup) and honestly
  marked as such; the run collected on the next session and passed. Acceptable once; the marking
  is what made it acceptable.
- **Gate A was skipped** and the tracker records the cost plainly: Phase 1 has no measurement of
  its own and never will. That is the one decision I would have made differently — it was cheap
  and it was the only uncontaminated datapoint the programme could ever have had.

## 8. Verdict

2.0 is a real improvement in **correctness**: the simulator can no longer reach a class of illegal
states, and the model is no longer trained on data with a ~45% corrupted lineup signal, collapsed
team labels in half its games, a phantom-third of the event-head positions, and a rotation target
that needed a 30% multiplier to mean anything. Those gains are structural and do not depend on
the train going well.

Whether it is an improvement in **results** is a forecast, and the forecast is: player-level
metrics improve, minutes most, with a real but not certain chance of crossing the season-average
baseline on minutes-driven stats after the dial refit; team-level metrics and win prediction stay
inside their noise. The dial package should come back smaller, and if it does not, that — not the
holdout MAE — is the signal to act on.

Push the branch, add the count dial, train, run the baseline comparison before anything else.
